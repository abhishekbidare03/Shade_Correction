# Pilot Proposal: Print Shade Correction at Batch Changeover

**To:** Special Projects Lead, Procurement · **From:** Abhishek, Forward Deployed Engineer · **Date:** 14 October 2026 · **Confidential**

Thank you for the discovery call. This note summarises what I propose, what I need from your
team, the cost, and when you can expect to see results.

### The issue as I understand it
When an SKU is re-run, the fired shade drifts from the master tile. The QC team corrects the
print file by eye, fires six to seven variants and repeats until the shade matches. Each round
takes six to seven hours while the line keeps producing off-shade tiles. Your lab already
measures how far a tile is from the master. What is missing is a reliable way to translate that
reading into the exact change to the print file.

### What I propose
A correction tool that works with your existing lab instrument, Photoshop workflow and staff:

1. Each master tile is measured once and stored as a numeric reference.
2. Each SKU is characterised once with eight planned test tiles.
3. At every batch changeover, the tool reads the first tile and calculates the correction within
   minutes. Your QC lead approves it, one confirmation tile is fired, and the batch is released.
4. The approved print file is never overwritten, which prevents the gradual drift seen today.

The pilot requires no cameras and no new production hardware. Every correction is approved by
your QC team, and where ink alone cannot fix a problem (for example, a darker glaze), the tool
reports it rather than suggesting a change that will not work.

### What I need from your team
- **Week 1:** lab readings of the master and sample tiles, one print file, and any past correction records.
- **A short meeting with the QC and pre-press leads,** who would use the tool day to day.
- **Confirmation of the pilot plant** (I suggest the UAE plant you mentioned), site access, and a few sample slots on the line.
- **Results measured by your own lab,** so the outcome is independent of our team.
